How your moves sound, now and proposed

Pick a district, then grind, do tricks and land, and switch between Now and Proposed while it plays. Both run on the game's own synth, over the district's real music. Nothing here is in the game yet.

A whole run

Four pushes on the beat, a jump, two tricks, a grind with a switch in the middle, off the rail on the beat, two more tricks and a clean landing. About twenty seconds. Play it once each way.

Grind

Now: a random line of even notes that doubles its rate with speed, the same shape in every district. Proposed: a written two-bar lick in the district's style and meter. Speed adds ornaments. Every two bars it develops: a second voice, a third higher, then bass and stabs. A switch turns the tune upside down. Leaving the rail plays a cadence, bigger the longer you held on.

Tricks and landing

Now: every trick is the same arpeggio, a little longer as the run grows; a missed one sounds like a good one. Proposed: each trick has its own figure, a run climbs the chord, from the third trick a second voice joins, a perfect one gets a high echo, a missed one stumbles. A clean landing after two or more tricks is a chord from the band; a sloppy one is a rim shot and a wrong note.

Pushing and jumping

Proposed: pushes on the beat count up, and the district's hand percussion follows the count. A jump on the beat gets one soft note. A long jump with no tricks climbs the chord, beat by beat, until you land.
